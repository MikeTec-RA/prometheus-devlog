The save that deleted things

September 10, 2026 · 2 min read

Contents
  1. Four ways to lose a scene
  2. The shape
  3. Three rules

This week I found four bugs that could permanently lose work. They looked unrelated. They turned out to be the same bug wearing four hats.

Four ways to lose a scene

  • A save that quietly dropped the scene's camera fly-through.
  • A loader that gave up waiting a moment too early on a cold start, and threw away every rail that objects were riding along.
  • An auto-save guard that pressing Pause switched off, so a running simulation, mid-tumble, could be saved over the scene you'd actually built.
  • An object whose model couldn't be found was skipped when the scene loaded, and the next auto-save, sixty seconds later, wrote the scene without it. Opening a scene while the backend was restarting was enough. No edit required.

The shape

Every one went the same way. Something failed to load, for a reason that wasn't its own fault: the server was restarting, a cache was cold, a load was still in flight. So it never made it into the live scene. Then a save came along, looked at the live scene, and wrote down what it saw.

To a save, something that failed to load looks exactly like something you deleted on purpose. Nothing complains on either side. The load logs a warning and carries on, and the save looks perfectly normal. You find out when the thing is gone.

Three rules

  1. Missing isn't the same as unavailable. "This doesn't exist" and "I couldn't reach it just now" are different answers, and only the first is allowed to remove anything. A comment in the code puts it best: collapsing the two is what let "a backend that was merely restarting look like a mass deletion."
  2. Hold, don't drop. If something can't be loaded, keep its saved record and write it back out unchanged. It's missing from the screen, which is honest. It isn't missing from the file.
  3. Teach the guard you already have. There was already a check that stops a half-loaded scene being saved over a full one. Teaching it about these cases beat bolting a second guard on beside it.

All four bugs are fixed. The rules stay: anything new that saves data gets asked what the next save writes when its load goes wrong.
